## Acceptance criteria for the residual work

Behavioural criteria are already met and proven by the tests cited above; they
are not repeated here. These remain open:

- The missing URL is absent from sitemap, canonical and hreflang output, not
  merely marked `noindex`.
- The global fallback's recovery control uses the same design-system control as
  the localized view, or the exception is recorded with its reason.
- No horizontal overflow or clipped content at 320, 390, 430, 768 and 1440 px.
- Light and dark themes meet WCAG AA for text, UI and focus contrast.
- Visual regression covers CS/EN × mobile/tablet/desktop × light/dark, plus the
  primary CTA focus state.

## Verification for the residual work

- Viewports: 320, 390, 430, 768, 1440.
- Themes: light, dark, forced colors where supported.
- SEO artifacts: generated sitemap, canonical and hreflang output for a missing
  URL.
- Quality gates: format, typecheck, lint, unit tests, route tests, E2E, build
  and visual regression.

## Explicit non-goals

- Do not reuse the editorial `Content State / Error` component as the route-level 404.
- Do not convert Payload/server exceptions into Not Found responses.
- Do not create one-off colors, spacing, icons, navigation or buttons.
- Do not redirect every unknown URL to the homepage; preserve HTTP 404 semantics.
- Do not add search, animation or illustration until the core recovery flow is verified.

## Dead ends and caveats

- A locale-scoped `not-found.tsx` alone may not cover completely unmatched URLs; test the root/global fallback explicitly.
- A Not Found result produced after streaming can retain HTTP 200; resolve existence before streamed Suspense boundaries where necessary.
- Global Not Found support and required file shape depend on the installed Next.js version/configuration. Verify the actual dependency before choosing `global-not-found` versus the root boundary.
- Do not infer missing content from a caught Payload exception. Null/not-published data and operational failures require different paths.
